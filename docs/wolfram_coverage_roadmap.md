## Method

* **Universe**: every symbol linked from the Wolfram Language *Alphabetical Listing* (`guide/AlphabeticalListing`), the documented built-ins (functions, options, constants and system variables).
* **Area**: the Documentation Center's 23 top-level areas. Each guide page linked from an area (and the guides linked from those, two levels deep) is assigned to that area; a symbol's area is the one whose guides list it most often. Symbols listed on no guide page (mostly options and `$` variables) are grouped under *Options, system variables & unclassified*.
* **Priority** (usage frequency proxy): **P1** = linked at least twice from *An Elementary Introduction to the Wolfram Language* (3rd ed.), or listed on a core-language guide page (lists, strings, associations, functional programming, patterns, rules, scoping, testing, elementary maths) and either used in that book or in the Core Language or Strings areas; **P2** = other symbols in the core computational areas, or used at least once in the book; **P3** = everything else that is feasible.
* **Infeasible**: symbols whose every guide page lies in an area that needs curated Wolfram data (geographic, scientific, engineering, financial, cultural and linguistic data, entities, natural-language understanding), the front end or notebooks (documents, UI construction), audio or video, trained ML models or LLM services, or the Wolfram Cloud; plus name-based overrides for such symbols listed in other areas (`*Data` functions, `Entity*`, `Cloud*`, `Notebook*`, `*Box`, `Audio*`, `Net*`, `LLM*`, ...), and 469 individually reviewed symbols listed with their reasons in `scripts/wolfram_data/infeasible_names.json`. Infeasible does not mean impossible: a local substitute (e.g. a small bundled dataset) could be added later, but it would not reproduce Wolfram's results.
* **Implemented**: the head has a `verified`, `fixed`, `promoted`, `kernel` or `added` status in `mikoshilang.library.STATUS` (each enforced by differential tests in `tests/library`), or is evaluated by the kernel itself (builtins, held special forms and structural heads such as `List`, `Rule`, `Function` and pattern objects). Quarantined and error heads count as missing.
